\begin{learningtargetbox}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} state the \textbf{zero product property} and explain why only the product
        \emph{zero} tells you anything about the factors.
  \item \ldots{} \textbf{solve a polynomial equation by factoring} --- standard form first, factor
        completely, then set each factor equal to zero.
  \item \ldots{} say how \textbf{many} solutions an equation has and what \textbf{kind} they are
        (real or imaginary), and write a factored equation \textbf{from} its solutions or from the
        $x$-intercepts of a graph.
  \item \ldots{} interpret solutions in context and \textbf{reject} the ones the situation forbids.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
This lesson turns four lessons of factoring into a way to \textbf{solve} (\texttt{A2.EI.2b},
\texttt{A2.EI.6a--b}). Nothing about factoring changes --- what changes is that the polynomial is
now set equal to \textbf{zero}, and zero is the only number that forces a factor to be anything in
particular. Three habits carry the day: \textbf{standard form first} (the property works only
against zero), \textbf{never divide both sides by a variable} (it throws away the solution $x=0$),
and \textbf{check every solution against the story} --- a negative time is arithmetic that the
situation still rejects.
\end{remindbox}
