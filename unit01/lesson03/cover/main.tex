\begin{learningtargetbox}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=5pt]
  \item \ldots{} find the \textbf{greatest common factor} of a polynomial in one or two variables
        and pull it out front.
  \item \ldots{} factor a \textbf{four-term} polynomial \textbf{by grouping}, including when a pair
        needs a negative factored out.
  \item \ldots{} factor \textbf{completely} --- GCF first, then group --- and check my answer by
        multiplying back.
  \item \ldots{} read a factored area or volume model to recover the \textbf{dimensions} of the
        figure it describes, and say what those dimensions mean.
\end{enumerate}
\end{learningtargetbox}

\begin{remindbox}
\small
This lesson covers \texttt{A2.EO.3b} --- factoring polynomials completely, in one and two
variables, with no more than four terms. Everything here is Lesson 1.1's distributive property run
backwards, and it carries Lesson 1.2's warning with it: pull out the \textbf{largest} shared piece,
not the first one you notice. Two habits matter most: \textbf{GCF first, every time}, and
\textbf{check by multiplying back}.
\end{remindbox}
