\begin{scenariobox}[The Scenario]{royal}
\small
Every question today is answered by reading a graph. \textbf{The rule for the whole activity: every
time you name an asymptote, write it as an \emph{equation} of a line --- $y=$ something or $x=$
something --- never as a bare number.} And whenever you are tempted to say a graph ``reaches'' a
value, check whether it really gets there or only gets closer. \textbf{Everyone starts at Tier R.}
When your whole group can do Tier R without help, move on.
\end{scenariobox}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=4pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Read the graph and fill in the table. Then describe the end behavior in words: as $t$ runs
        off to the right, what do the outputs do?
\smallskip

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}|l|c|c|c|c|c|@{}}
\hline
$t$ (hours) & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ \\ \hline
$D(t)$ (mg) & \ans{$64$} & \ans{$32$} & \ans{$16$} & \ans{$8$} & \ans{$4$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\ansline{As $t \to \infty$ the outputs level off toward $0$ --- they keep halving and stay positive.}\\

  \item Give the horizontal asymptote as an \emph{equation}, and say what that line means about the
        medicine.

\ansline{The horizontal asymptote is $y=0$ (the line, not the bare number $0$).}\\
\ansline{The amount in the blood keeps shrinking toward zero but never becomes exactly zero.}\\

  \item Answer each one, with a reason in a few words.
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
          \item Does $D$ have a zero?
          \item Does $D$ have an absolute maximum? If so, give its value and location.
          \item Does $D$ have an absolute minimum?
        \end{enumerate}

\ansline{(a) No. $D(t)$ is positive for every $t$, so no input makes the output $0$.}\\
\ansline{(b) Yes: $64$ mg, at $t=0$ --- an endpoint, the moment the dose was taken.}\\
\ansline{(c) No. Every hour is lower than the one before, so no output is the lowest one.}\\

  \item The bottle says the medicine is \emph{``out of your system in 24 hours.''} Using this graph,
        is that literally true? Write one sentence a pharmacist could say instead that is both honest
        and useful.

\ansline{Not literally. By this model some medicine is always left --- the amount approaches $0$}\\
\ansline{without reaching it. Honest version: ``After 24 hours less than a millionth of the dose is}\\
\ansline{left, which is far too little to have any effect.'' Small enough to ignore is not zero.}\\
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=4pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Graph (a).} Read the four values off the graph, then describe the end behavior as
        $w$ runs off to the right and give the horizontal asymptote as an equation.
\smallskip

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}|l|c|c|c|c|@{}}
\hline
$w$ (weeks) & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\ \hline
$S(w)$ (wpm) & \ans{$16$} & \ans{$48$} & \ans{$64$} & \ans{$72$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\ansline{As $w \to \infty$ the speed levels off toward $80$ words per minute --- the gains keep}\\
\ansline{halving. The horizontal asymptote is $y=80$.}\\

  \item \textbf{Graph (a), the two questions that matter.} Will this student ever type $80$ words per
        minute? Will they ever type $85$? Answer each and give a different reason for each.

\ansline{$80$: no. The graph approaches $y=80$ and never reaches it --- closer is not there.}\\
\ansline{$85$: no, and for a stronger reason. $85$ is \emph{above} the asymptote; the graph never}\\
\ansline{even gets to $80$, so it is nowhere near crossing above it. Two different ``no''s.}\\

  \item \textbf{Graph (a).} Does $S$ have an absolute maximum? Does it have an absolute minimum? For
        each, give a value and a location or explain why there is none.

\ansline{No absolute maximum: the outputs climb toward $80$ without any output being the highest.}\\
\ansline{Absolute minimum $16$ wpm at $w=0$ --- an endpoint, the week practice started.}\\

  \item \textbf{Graph (b).} Give both asymptotes as equations, give the domain of $h$, and describe
        the end behavior at both ends.

\ansline{Vertical asymptote $x=-2$; horizontal asymptote $y=0$.}\\
\ansline{Domain: every real number except $-2$ --- the one input with no point on the graph.}\\
\ansline{As $x \to \infty$ the outputs level off toward $0$ from above; as $x \to -\infty$, toward $0$ from below.}\\

  \item \textbf{Graph (b), the transformation question.} The parent here is $y=\dfrac{4}{x}$, whose
        asymptotes are $x=0$ and $y=0$. Graph (b) is that parent moved \emph{left $2$} (Lesson 2.2).
        Which asymptote moved, which one did not, and why?

\ansline{The \emph{vertical} asymptote moved, $x=0 \to x=-2$. The horizontal one stayed at $y=0$.}\\
\ansline{A horizontal slide changes \emph{inputs}, and a vertical asymptote is named by an input, so}\\
\ansline{it travels. A horizontal line is named by an output, and no output changed --- 2.2's rule.}\\
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black!40, title=\textbf{Tier E --- Extension},
    fonttitle=\bfseries, arc=2mm, left=3mm, right=3mm, top=2mm, bottom=2mm, breakable]
\small

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=4pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Two claims, and each one confuses ``close'' with ``there.''} Name the mistake and give
        the correct statement.
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
          \item About the coffee graph in your notes: \emph{``The coffee reaches $20^\circ$C at about
                $t=8$ hours, because the curve is sitting right on the dashed line there.''}

\ansline{The curve is close enough to the line to look like it, not on it: at $t=8$ the gap is}\\
\ansline{$0.25^\circ$C. Correct: $C$ approaches $20^\circ$C and never reaches it. $y=20$ is an asymptote.}\\

          \item About $f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x-3}$ in your notes: \emph{``This function has a zero at
                $x=3$, because that is where the graph meets the vertical line.''}

\ansline{A zero and a vertical asymptote are opposites. At a zero the function \emph{has} a value and}\\
\ansline{that value is $0$; at $x=3$ the function has no value at all --- $3$ is not in the domain, so}\\
\ansline{there is no point there. The zero is $x=-1$, where the curve crosses the horizontal axis.}\\
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Possible or impossible?} For each description, either name a graph from today that
        does it, or explain why nothing can.
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
          \item Outputs that climb without bound on the right and level off toward a number on the
                left.

\ansline{Possible: $y=2^x$ (notes box 1, panel (c)). Levels toward $0$ on the left, climbs on the right.}\\

          \item Outputs that level off toward the same number at \emph{both} ends.

\ansline{Possible: $h(x)=\dfrac{4}{x+2}$ above, and $f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x-3}$ in the notes (toward $1$).}\\

          \item A graph that crosses its own \emph{vertical} asymptote.

\ansline{Impossible. A vertical asymptote sits at an input that is \emph{not in the domain}, so the}\\
\ansline{function has no output there and there is no point to plot --- nothing to cross with.}\\
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Where does the asymptote go?} The graph of $y=2^x$ has the horizontal asymptote
        $y=0$. Give the horizontal asymptote of each function below, then explain why the third one
        behaves differently from the first two.
\smallskip

\hspace{1.2em} (a) $y=2^x+3$ \qquad (b) $y=2^x-1$ \qquad (c) $y=2^{x-3}$

\ansline{(a) $y=3$ \qquad (b) $y=-1$ \qquad (c) $y=0$, unchanged.}\\
\ansline{(a) and (b) change every \emph{output}, so the line the outputs approach moves with them.}\\
\ansline{(c) is inside the exponent --- it changes \emph{inputs} and slides the graph sideways, and}\\
\ansline{sliding sideways cannot move a horizontal line. Outside moves it, inside does not (2.2).}\\
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
